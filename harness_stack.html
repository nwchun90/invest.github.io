AI models are no longer the story — the infrastructure beneath them is. Think of the AI ecosystem as a new global highway: NVIDIA builds the engines, Celestica lays the asphalt, and Oracle runs the tolls and traffic systems. You need all three for the system to work, and every major tech company on earth is racing to build more of it. That’s where the real investment story lives.
A note before we begin

This presentation uses the “AI highway” analogy to simplify a complex stack. The point isn’t the metaphor — it’s the structure underneath it. Compute, systems, and platforms form a single chain, and NVIDIA, Celestica, and Oracle each anchor one of those links. Keep that in mind as we walk through the numbers.

what is harness engineering?

The concept in plain English

An AI model on its own is like a powerful engine sitting in a field. It can do nothing useful until someone builds the car around it — the chassis, the wheels, the fuel system, the dashboard. That car is the harness: the tools, memory, networking, and infrastructure that let the model actually do work in the real world.

The companies winning the AI race in 2026 are not necessarily the ones with the smartest model. They are the ones building the best harness around it. NVIDIA provides the engine power. Celestica builds the chassis. Oracle provides the road, the fuel, and the navigation system. The model itself — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini — rides inside.

Why this matters for investors: Models are becoming interchangeable. The harness is not. A company that owns a critical layer of the harness has a durable competitive advantage that does not disappear when a new model comes along. That is what we are looking for in these three companies.

Where the money flows: Every major technology company — Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta — is spending hundreds of billions building this infrastructure. That spending flows directly to NVIDIA (chips), Celestica (assembly), and Oracle (cloud platform). The capex (capital expenditure — money spent building long-term physical assets like data centres and factories) cycle is the largest in technology history.

Check 2 of 3  ·  Is the spending disciplined?
Capital expenditure (capex) is money spent building long-term assets — factories, data centres, equipment. Heavy capex is necessary to grow, but too much relative to income is a warning sign. We use two ratios: capex as a share of revenue, and capex as a share of operating income.

where each company sits in the AI harness
LAYER 1 · THE ENGINE
NVDA — The chip inside everything
Every AI workload running anywhere in the world — from a chatbot answering a question to a military targeting system making a decision — runs on NVIDIA chips. The company does not make its own products physically; it designs the chips and TSMC manufactures them. This keeps costs low and margins extraordinary. The new Blackwell chip generation is now shipping; the next generation (Rubin) arrives later in 2026. No credible alternative exists at this scale today.
LAYER 2 · THE CHASSIS
CLS — The builder of AI's physical home
Celestica assembles the physical systems that house NVIDIA chips: AI servers, networking switches, and complete rack-scale platforms. It does not just build to a design — it co-designs the platforms alongside companies like AMD and Google, which is a much higher-value activity. The latest win (co-packaged optics switch, using fibre to connect AI systems at the speed of light) is a world first. Revenue guided at $19 billion for 2026 — up 53%. CEO called the order backlog "the strongest in my tenure."
LAYER 3 · THE PLATFORM
ORCL — The cloud home every AI model needs
Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) has quietly become the preferred data centre for AI's biggest names: OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, xAI, and Cohere all run workloads on Oracle's infrastructure. The company is spending aggressively to build capacity — $16 billion in capital expenditure this year — backed by $130 billion in signed customer contracts. Oracle also owns the database layer: the persistent storage and memory system that AI agents read from and write to when they work on long tasks.
THE RISK · FOR ALL THREE
What breaks the story?
For NVDA: a rival chip (AMD, custom Google/Amazon silicon) narrows the performance gap meaningfully. For CLS: a major customer builds its own assembly capability in-house. For ORCL: the hyperscaler giants (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud) retake the AI workloads that have migrated to OCI. None of these is likely in the next 12–18 months, but all three are worth watching over a 3–5 year horizon.
do they own an AI model — or are they tied to one?
An important question for the long run: does each company own its own frontier AI model (like ChatGPT or Claude), or does it depend on someone else's model to power its products? The answer shapes how much pricing power each company has in the future.

NVIDIA does not develop its own language model and does not need to. Every model lab — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, xAI — runs on NVIDIA hardware. NVIDIA wins regardless of which model wins the race. Perfect neutrality.

Celestica has no model and needs none. Its business is one layer below: assembling the hardware that runs the chips that run the models. As long as AI exists and requires physical infrastructure, Celestica has a role.

Oracle is the most interesting case. It does not develop its own frontier model, but it has contractual relationships with every major model provider simultaneously. Oracle is the landlord; the model companies are tenants paying rent in the form of cloud contracts. This is a powerful position — Oracle benefits from all of them without betting on any one of them.
My read on this

The harness engineering thesis is really a simple idea dressed in technical language: when a new technology becomes essential infrastructure, the companies that build and maintain that infrastructure tend to win over the long run — even more reliably than the companies making the flashiest product on top of it. NVIDIA, Celestica, and Oracle are infrastructure plays. The AI model companies grabbing headlines are the tenants. I'd rather own the building.

the short version
All three companies pass the three financial health checks, each with a different risk profile. NVDA is the most financially sound — exceptional margins, minimal capital spending, and $57 billion in annual free cash flow. CLS is growing the fastest relative to its starting point, with the most direct exposure to the physical AI infrastructure buildout. ORCL carries the heaviest capital spending burden but has the most concrete evidence of future demand — $130 billion in signed contracts already on the books.

Together: These three companies occupy three non-overlapping layers of the same infrastructure stack. Owning all three is not three bets on AI — it is one coherent bet, spread across the engine, the chassis, and the platform.
