what accumulation and distribution actually look like

The market hides institutional intent in plain sight. Two volume patterns reveal it — and they run opposite to what feels natural.

✓ Accumulation — Bullish
Down days: Low volume — weak sellers, no real panic
Up days: High volume — strong buyers stepping in
Price: Sideways or mild downtrend — looks unremarkable
Headlines: Quiet. Nothing to get excited about.
This is where the next move begins.
✗ Distribution — Bearish
Down days: High volume — institutions selling into strength
Up days: Low volume — retail buying, no real conviction
Price: Near recent highs — looks exciting
Headlines: Positive news, analyst upgrades, social buzz
This is where the last buyer is found.
three quick filters before any trade
Check the sector first. Institutions rarely buy one stock — they rotate into sectors. If the broader sector is under distribution, an individual name is unlikely to run regardless of how cheap it looks. The tide always matters more than the fish.
Test the pullback. A healthy dip in an uptrend comes on shrinking volume and finds support near the 20- or 50-day moving average. A breakdown comes on swelling volume and keeps going. Volume tells you which one you are in — price alone does not.
Good news + flat or falling price = red flag. When an earnings beat or major announcement fails to push the price higher, institutions are using the excitement to sell into retail demand. If everyone in your group chat is talking about it, distribution is already underway.

From intuition to numbers
About 65–70% of what you just read can be turned into a formula.
Volume flow, price relative to its own weighted average, how tightly a stock tracks its sector, whether price dips attract buying volume — all of these are mechanically measurable from publicly available daily data. The remaining 30–35% is judgment: reading macro context, weighing news quality, sensing whether a rotation is structural or temporary.

The five components below — together called the IA_Score — capture the quantifiable portion. Think of it not as a buy signal, but as a pre-filter that tells you whether the technical conditions for institutional accumulation are currently in place. The formula removes the emotion. You still supply the conviction.
the ia_score — five signals, one number

Each component is normalised to a value between 0 and 1. The IA_Score is their simple average. A score near 1.0 means all five signals confirm institutional buying. A score near 0 means the opposite.

CMF
Money Flow
Chaikin Money Flow — measures whether volume is flowing into or out of the asset over a 20-period rolling window
OBV
Volume Trend
On-Balance Volume ratio — whether cumulative buying volume is running above its own 50-period moving average
VWAP
Price Level
Price relative to its 20-period volume-weighted average — confirms that price strength is backed by real participation
Alignment
Sector Sync
20-period rolling correlation with the sector benchmark — confirms the stock is moving with institutional rotation, not against it
⑤ Dip Recovery Whether a recent price trough was met with genuine buying — measured as the ratio of bounce volume to sell-off volume in the 10-period window surrounding the most recent low.
Final score:   IA_Score = (CMF + OBV + VWAP + Alignment + Dip Recovery) ÷ 5

the mathematics — for those who want to verify every component

How Each Component Is Calculated

① CMF — Chaikin Money Flow (20 periods)
MF = [(Close − Low) − (High − Close)] / (High − Low)
CMF = Σ(MF × Volume, 20) / Σ(Volume, 20)
CMF_N = (CMF + 1) / 2
Maps native range [−1, +1] to [0, 1]. +1 = all capital flowing in.
② OBV — On-Balance Volume (vs SMA 50)
OBV = cumulative signed volume
   (+Volume if Close ↑ · −Volume if Close ↓)
OBV_N = min(1, OBV / SMA(OBV, 50))
Capped at 1.0 — penalises weakness, never punishes strength.
③ VWAP — Volume-Weighted Price (20 periods)
Typical = (High + Low + Close) / 3
VWAP = Σ(Typical × Volume, 20) / Σ(Volume, 20)
VWAP_N = min(1, Close / VWAP)
Price at or above VWAP signals buyers are in control.
④ Alignment — Sector Correlation (20 periods)
R_asset = Close / Close_prev − 1
R_sector = Sector_Close / Sector_prev − 1
Align_N = max(0, CORREL(R_asset, R_sector, 20))
Floors at 0 — negative correlation treated as no signal, not a penalty.
⑤ Dip Recovery — Bounce vs Sell-off Volume
Trough = lowest close in last 10 periods
Selloff_Vol = total volume · 5 bars before trough
Bounce_Vol = total volume · 5 bars after trough
Recovery_N = min(1, Bounce_Vol / Selloff_Vol)
= 1 when bounce volume fully matched or exceeded the sell-off. Returns 0.5 when no prior sell-off is detected.
Final Score
IA_Score = (CMF_N + OBV_N + VWAP_N
             + Align_N + Recovery_N) / 5
Range: 0.0 (all weak) to 1.0 (all strong). Simple unweighted average.

All lookback counts are in bars. The default implementation fetches hourly (1h) OHLCV data for a daily analysis cadence — so a "20-period" window covers approximately 20 trading hours. Data source: Yahoo Finance via yfinance. A sector benchmark (e.g. an ETF tracking the relevant industry) is required for the Alignment component.

the one rule that changes everything

"Institutions don't chase. They position. By the time the news reaches you, the trade is over."

The IA_Score does not predict the future. It answers a narrower question: are the technical conditions for institutional accumulation present right now? A high score tells you who is holding the ball. It does not tell you when they will throw it. Use it as a pre-filter — one more lens before your own judgment makes the final call.
