Gold’s slide from $5,602 to $4,190 — and its rebound above $4,600 — isn’t just volatility. It’s the imprint of the US–Iran war pressed straight onto the chart. In sixty days, the market moved through three clean phases: a fear‑driven spike, a supply‑shock collapse, and a double‑bottom reset. Each phase had a cause. Each one still echoes in today’s price.

four phases

the paradox — why conflict caused gold to fall

The Hormuz transmission mechanism

Phase 1 — expected: War begins → safe-haven demand → gold to ATH $5,602. The textbook response lasted 48 hours.

Phase 2 — the twist: Hormuz closes → oil +55% → inflation spikes → Fed holds rates → real yields stay elevated → opportunity cost of non-yielding gold rises → sell-off.

Phase 3 — reassertion: Once the closure proved structural, gold shifted from short-term trade to long-term insurance. Central banks and sovereign funds re-entered. RSI ~20 signalled selling exhaustion.

The double bottom: Two tests of ~$4,190 with RSI at extreme oversold confirmed exhaustion. The neckline at ~$4,730 was the pivotal level — a confirmed close above it re-established the medium-term uptrend.

verdict
The gold chart from February to today is one of the most instructive macro case studies of the decade — not because of what gold did, but because of why. The initial surge to $5,602 was the expected safe-haven response. The collapse to $4,190 was not weakness — it was the logical result of the Hormuz closure transmitting through oil prices into inflation and then into rate expectations. The double bottom and recovery were gold's structural thesis reasserting against that headwind, supported by central bank demand that does not pause for geopolitical news.

Current positioning: At $4,613, gold sits above the double-bottom support zone but below the April recovery peak. The RSI at 43–49 is neutral — not a strong entry signal. The cleaner entry would be on a confirmed Islamabad breakdown (Hormuz stays closed → gold retests $4,200–4,400 on safe-haven re-entry) or on a CPI undershoot (Fed can cut → rate headwind eases → gold rallies toward $5,000).
