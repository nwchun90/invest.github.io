The Rule That Changed Everything — March 30, 2026

Old Rule — Before May 1, 2026

3-month minimum seasoning. Even a trillion-dollar company had to sit on the sidelines after its IPO before it could be evaluated for Nasdaq-100 inclusion. In practice, this often stretched closer to a year. Index ETFs — and every investor holding them — simply couldn't own a new listing until well after the excitement had settled.

New Rule — Effective May 1, 2026

15 trading days. Nasdaq adopted a Fast Entry provision on March 30, 2026, specifically designed for mega-cap IPOs. If a newly listed company ranks within the top 40 constituents by full market cap, it is added to the Nasdaq-100 within 15 trading days — no seasoning, no liquidity test, no waiting for December reconstitution. SpaceX reportedly made rapid inclusion a condition of listing on Nasdaq.

Why this matters for passive investors: Before this rule, holding a Nasdaq-100 ETF meant waiting months before any new AI giant appeared in your portfolio. Now, the index — and every ETF tracking it — absorbs these companies automatically within three weeks. You don't need to predict IPO dates, negotiate allocations, or trade anything. The index mechanism does it for you.

etf exposure — buffered, not risk-free
What the ETF protects you from
Day-one chaos. You skip the first 15 trading days — the most volatile window. IPO-day swings of ±20% are common. You enter after some price discovery has already occurred.
Allocation uncertainty. No broker access needed, no waitlists, no minimum investment. Once SpaceX is in the index, you hold it proportionally — automatically.
Concentration risk. SpaceX enters at ~0.5% index weight (limited free float). Even if the IPO craters, 99 other holdings absorb the impact. You are not betting the portfolio on a single debut.
Risks that remain — be honest about them
Forced buying. When SpaceX enters the index, every Nasdaq-100 ETF must buy it at whatever market price exists that day. If the stock is overvalued at inclusion, the ETF buys it anyway — at that price.
Lock-up expiry. 90–180 days after IPO, insiders can sell. This happens after ETF inclusion — and large insider sales can drag the stock and, modestly, the index.
You miss the pop. If SpaceX doubles in its first 15 trading days, the ETF captures none of it. The buffered entry cuts both ways — less chaos, but also less first-mover upside.
The honest summary: A Nasdaq-100 ETF gives you passive, automatic, diversified exposure to SpaceX — and every AI giant that follows — without needing to predict IPO dates or fight for allocations. It is not a risk-free structure; it is a risk-buffered one. The volatility is muted by diversification, the entry is delayed by 15 days, and the long-run compounding of the index does the work. For most investors, that trade-off is the point.

The dividend withholding note
US-listed ETFs like QQQM pay quarterly dividends. Held outside a qualifying retirement account, the IRS withholds 15% of every dividend at source — a drag that compounds silently over decades. Inside a treaty-protected retirement account, this withholding drops to zero.
HXQ sidesteps this entirely through its swap structure. Because it pays no distributions, there is no dividend event for any tax authority to withhold on. The cost is embedded in the fund's economics — not deducted from yours. This is the core reason HXQ suits tax-free and taxable accounts while QQQM suits retirement accounts.

verdict
If you are not in the SpaceX IPO — and most investors won't be — the Nasdaq-100 is the cleanest available path to owning it. The Fast Entry rule (May 2026) means the index will hold SpaceX within 15 trading days of its debut, automatically, in both QQQM and HXQ. Neither approach requires you to predict an IPO date, fight for an allocation, or time a volatile post-IPO trade. QQQM in a retirement account eliminates dividend withholding. HXQ in a tax-free or taxable account eliminates dividend complexity entirely. The choice between them is structural — not a view on which ETF will perform better. Both track the same index.
